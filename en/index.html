📖 How to use

What does this tool do?

It shrinks a single image's file size while preserving visual quality. Typical uses: meeting upload caps on forms and portals, getting under email attachment limits, speeding up web pages, and freeing up phone storage.

Steps

  1. Upload one image — click, drag & drop, or paste with Ctrl+V.
  2. It compresses automatically — original and result appear side by side.
  3. Tune it (each change recompresses instantly):
    · Quality — drag the slider (1–100). Lower means smaller and softer; 80 is the sweet spot.
    · Target size — type "under 200 KB" and the tool finds the best quality that fits.
    · Output format — Auto (WebP, 25–35% smaller than JPEG), JPEG, or PNG.
    · Max dimension — set a cap to scale down and save much more.
  4. Download — click "Download image" to get one compressed file (named with a _min suffix).

What to expect

  • Phone photos (3–5 MB) → usually 300–800 KB, an 80–90% saving
  • Web images (500 KB–1 MB) → usually 100–250 KB, a 70–80% saving
  • Already-optimised images (<200 KB) → modest gains, maybe 10–20% or even slightly larger

FAQ

Q: Are my images uploaded to a server?
A: No. Everything runs in your browser. Images never leave your device, and it works offline.

Q: Why only one image at a time?
A: To keep the workflow dead simple — upload one, download one. No ZIP, no unzipping, no partial-batch failures. Just repeat for more images.

Q: Will quality suffer?
A: Quality 80 (the default) is visually near-lossless for most photos — and you can compare original vs. result side by side. Use 90–95 when quality is critical; 70 is fine for social media.

Q: Why didn't PNG shrink much?
A: PNG is lossless, so there is little to reclaim. Switch to WebP or JPEG, or set a max dimension to scale down.

Q: Why did my file get bigger?
A: If the original was already highly optimised, re-encoding can add a little. The tool tells you explicitly in that case — just keep your original.

Q: Is EXIF data (like GPS location) preserved?
A: No. Redrawing on Canvas drops EXIF metadata, which conveniently also removes location privacy data from your photos.
