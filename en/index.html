📖 How to use

What does this tool do?

It shrinks image file size while preserving visual quality. Typical uses: speeding up website loading, reducing email attachment size, meeting upload size limits on forms and portals, and freeing up phone storage.

Steps

  1. Add images — click the upload area, drag in multiple files, or paste with Ctrl+V.
  2. Pick a mode:
    · Quality — drag the slider (1–100). Lower means smaller and softer. 80 is the sweet spot.
    · Target size — type "under 200 KB" and the tool finds the best quality that fits.
  3. Choose an output format
    · Auto (recommended) — WebP, which is 25–35% smaller than JPEG at equal quality.
    · JPEG — maximum compatibility for legacy systems.
    · PNG — lossless with transparency, but limited compression (resize helps most).
  4. (Optional) Cap the dimensions — set a max longest side to scale down and save much more.
  5. Download — individually, or use "Download all" to get a ZIP.

What to expect

  • Phone photos (3–5 MB) → usually 300–800 KB, a 80–90% saving
  • Web images (500 KB–1 MB) → usually 100–250 KB, a 70–80% saving
  • Already-optimised images (<200 KB) → modest gains, maybe 10–20%

FAQ

Q: Are my images uploaded to a server?
A: No. Everything runs in your browser. Images never leave your device, and it works offline.

Q: Will quality suffer?
A: Quality 80 (the default) is visually near-lossless for most photos. Use 90–95 when quality is critical; 70 is fine for social media.

Q: Why didn't PNG shrink much?
A: PNG is lossless, so there is little to reclaim. Switch to WebP or JPEG, or set a max dimension to scale down.

Q: Why did some files get bigger?
A: If the original was already highly optimised, re-encoding can add a little. The tool flags these as "Larger" — just keep your original in that case.

Q: Is EXIF data (like GPS location) preserved?
A: No. Redrawing on Canvas drops EXIF metadata, which conveniently also removes location privacy data from your photos.

Q: How many files can I process?
A: No hard limit, but memory-bound. Keep batches under ~100, or split very large images.
